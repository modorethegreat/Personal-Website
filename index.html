    Engineering
    What changed when the grip moved off-center?

    I compared flat and V-groove jaws on the same MuJoCo lift-and-hold task. With a cube offset by 3 mm at a sliding friction coefficient of 0.11, the flat jaw held above the 80 mm clearance threshold and the V-groove fell below it. I saw the same pass/fail split when I halved the timestep.

    This was one simulated setup with simplified contacts. The replay pairs the same two trials so I can see when contact changes and where the clearance starts to diverge.

    Physics
    What should a physics compiler preserve?

    When two models share an interface, the solver should know which quantities exchange power and how the discrete steps account for it. I’m exploring how discrete exterior calculus and port-Hamiltonian models could express those obligations while keeping topology, metric choices and material laws distinct.

    A proposed first benchmark is a coupled thermal interface. Check equal-and-opposite transfer across the interface and track the energy balance over time, then compare the result with an analytic reference.

    Engineering
    When should a simulation result go stale?

    A run can be perfectly reproducible and still answer an earlier version of the question. In the AeroG workbench prototype, results retain their input and geometry revisions. Editing a requirement withholds the old assessment until a matching run can be reviewed.

    That distinction matters in a diagnostic study. Passing conservation and mesh-sensitivity checks establishes evidence for the modeled case. Hardware qualification calls for a different body of evidence.

    A small test for scientific machine learning

    In inverse materials modeling, I start with a stress–strain response from known parameters and ask whether the model can recover them. The synthetic case gives me a known answer and helps expose errors in units, loss terms, or parameter bounds before moving to measured data.

    Research
    What can open permit data actually tell us?

    Open records can show permit volumes and some timing patterns. I looked at what they could support for a municipal permit intake tool. Without drawings and reviewer comments, they cannot explain why an application was returned or establish how much review time the tool would save.

    The next validation step would be to compare common completeness problems with the checks a jurisdiction actually performs.

    Research
    Designing a task where information has a cost

    An experiment concept on boredom and exploration uses a small reward grid and a limited move budget. Revealing a reward spends a move that could otherwise collect points. The software design would record each choice and the information visible at that moment.

    Allowing a participant to choose an unrevealed reward would add a different question about uncertainty. This is an experiment design with no participant results yet.
